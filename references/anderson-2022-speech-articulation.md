# Articulatory descriptions in Essentials of Linguistics

## Key takeaway

The textbook supplies complementary dimensions for describing speech sounds rather than treating letters, articulation and musical pitch as interchangeable.

## Summary

Selected sections of [Essentials of Linguistics 2e](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) support the articulatory feature Model. Its singing examples are contextual applications, not a complete diction curriculum.

## Located contributions

[Section 3.2](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/3-2-speech-articulators/) identifies speech articulators. [Section 3.3](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/3-3-describing-consonants-place-and-phonation/) distinguishes consonant place and voicing. [Section 3.4](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/3-4-describing-consonants-manner/) explains selected manners and oral/nasal routing.

[Section 3.5 in the credited republication](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Linguistics/Essentials_of_Linguistics_2e_%28Anderson_et_al.%29/03%3A_Phonetics/3.05%3A_Describing_vowels) supplies vowel height, backness, rounding and nasality. The original chapter URL returned 403 during this inspection. The mirror is the same publication family, not independent corroboration.

## Limits and adaptations

Only the stated text portions were inspected for this contribution. No source chart, recording, complete IPA inventory, tenseness theory or language-specific pronunciation system is adopted. Broad categories vary in their realisation across speakers and languages. The local nasal/stop and vowel/pitch examples use those distinctions without prescribing a vocal posture or claiming health or learning effects. Timing, lexical stress and word meaning need further accounts.
