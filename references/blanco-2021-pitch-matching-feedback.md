# Blanco and colleagues on pitch matching feedback

## Key takeaway

A laboratory reference-replay task supports a comparison procedure, not a guarantee that ear-only repetition teaches accurate singing.

## Summary

Blanco, Tassani and Ramirez (2021), Frontiers in Psychology 12:684693, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693. [Publisher article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full), re-inspected 2026-10-16 within the narrower metadata scope.

## Located contribution

Section 2.3 Baseline describes listening, attempting and hearing the reference again. Section 2.4.1 measures pitch error, normalises octave differences and applies a study-specific correctness cutoff. Sections 3 and 4 distinguish visual feedback, own-timbre auditory feedback and control conditions; gains and transfer differ by condition. Repeated reference listening alone is not established as an effective training solution.

## Application and limits

Our Method retains reference replay but uses brief comfortable attempts and qualitative reports. It does not import range sweeps, laboratory timing, octave-normalised scoring or the cutoff. The Practice explicitly treats an octave difference as a different height. Selected participants, synthesized targets and short experimental transfer do not establish long-term beginner learning, universal vocal suitability or the effectiveness of this phone-only guide. No raw data were reanalysed.
