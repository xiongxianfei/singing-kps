# Auditory vocal pitch matching

## Key takeaway

Hearing a target, organising a vocal action and interpreting the resulting sound are related but distinct functions.

## Summary

This is a beginner-facing subset of the proposed architecture in Pfordresher and colleagues (2015), Figure 1. It explains why hearing that notes differ does not automatically mean being able to reproduce one. It represents functions and information flow, not directly observed parts of this learner's brain or a proven single cause of inaccurate singing.

## Entities and relationships

| Part | Relevant content | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| External reference | The played pitch, duration and tone colour | Supplies information to perception |
| Perceptual representation | The heard pitch distinguished from other sound properties | Supplies a goal that must be related to vocal actions |
| Sensorimotor translation | Associations between heard results and ways of producing them | Connects perceived pitch to a motor plan |
| Motor plan and vocal output | Coordination of respiration, phonation and articulation; the resulting sound | Produces sound that can return as auditory feedback |
| Auditory feedback | The heard result of one's own vocal action | Re-enters perception; it is not automatically a reliable correctness label |

Read the chain as **external sound -> perception -> translation -> motor planning and output -> auditory feedback -> perception**. These arrows mean proposed functional connections, not guaranteed improvement with every circuit. The source's broader architecture also includes categorical pitch representations and long-term memory influencing perception and planning. This subset omits that detailed tonal pathway and neural/developmental accounts.

## Applying the representation

In a constructed case, a learner notices that their attempt is lower but cannot yet produce a closer note. The model distinguishes interpreting a discrepancy from organising a correction; recognising the first does not establish mastery of the second. Repeatedly guessing higher is therefore not a diagnosis or a justified guaranteed solution.

For a short reference-replay task, the learner also has to retain the target during the pause. Replaying may refresh it, but a mismatch cannot tell us whether the difficulty concerned retaining, perceiving, producing or comparing it. This task-level explanation is our application, not an added memory module claimed to have been measured.

## Assumptions and limits

The source explicitly proposes a framework rather than a complete causal test. This diagram-free account does not represent all feedback paths, bodily sensations, health conditions, musical traditions or contexts. It predicts no individual's range or rate of learning. Teaching and clinical assessment answer different questions; the model does not replace either.

## Interpretation check

Could someone hear a difference yet have trouble changing their voice? Yes: the representation separates those functions. Does one unsuccessful attempt identify a defective component? No: multiple parts and their connections could contribute, and no diagnostic measurements were made.
