# Singing subject map

## Key takeaway

Locate a singing question in the wider subject, then see the explanation available here and the gaps beside it. The current library supports a first beginner task, not every topic in this map.

## Summary

This authored outline brings together voice pedagogy, musical understanding and practice. It selects established subjects for a beginner-facing library and aligns current explanations with them. The subject divisions describe knowledge; [First singing session](../practices/first-singing-session.md) owns its activity sequence and entry conditions. Topics can share explanations across the five KPS role folders.

## Scope and reading

The implemented scope remains comfortable voice awareness, pitch direction, beat and a short humming task. The broader outline provides orientation for questions and future development; a gap is not an instruction to attempt an unfamiliar technique or a commitment to build a complete curriculum. Clinical assessment, rehabilitation, professional voice teaching and comprehensive coverage of musical traditions are outside this package.

**Partial** means the linked local explanation covers only part of the topic. **Source only** means a Reference identifies relevant external material but no local lesson is supplied. **Gap** means the needed local explanation is absent. A syllabus topic establishes a place in an outline; it does not establish a mechanism, prescribe an exercise or demonstrate effectiveness.

The [Practice starting conditions](../practices/first-singing-session.md#starting-conditions) govern vocal activity. The map does not change those boundaries. It is possible to explore the subject without performing its vocal tasks.

## Subject outline

The rows are subject groupings, not lessons to complete in order. The ten-part grouping is our synthesis of the source structures identified below, not a universal singing taxonomy.

| Topic | What it covers and connects to | Existing explanations |
|---|---|---|
| Voice production and breathing | Breath, vocal-fold vibration and body coordination; connects the sound source to resonance and phrase demands | **Partial:** [Voice production](../concepts/voice-production.md) introduces the mechanism. **Gap:** a fuller breathing and coordination account. |
| Vocal health and comfortable use | Voice care and the limits of self-observation; connects every vocal task to its entry and stop conditions | [Voice-care Reference](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md); [Practice starting conditions](../practices/first-singing-session.md#starting-conditions) and [comfort check](../practices/first-singing-session.md#stage1-check-voice-comfort). These do not provide diagnosis or individual clearance. |
| Listening pitch and intonation | Hearing pitch direction and comparing an intended pitch with a reference; connects listening to melody and feedback | **Partial:** [Pitch](../concepts/pitch.md) and [pitch-direction task](../practices/first-singing-session.md#stage2-explore-pitch-direction). **Gap:** reference-pitch matching, intervals and tuning explanations. A rise-and-return contour is not a tuning measurement. |
| Beat rhythm and meter | Pulse, duration, silence and beat grouping; connects musical time to sung patterns | **Partial:** [Beat and rhythm](../concepts/beat-and-rhythm.md) and [four-beat task](../practices/first-singing-session.md#stage3-feel-four-beats). [Rhythm](../references/open-music-theory-undated-notating-rhythm.md) and [meter](../references/open-music-theory-undated-simple-meter.md) References identify source contributions. **Gap:** a local explanation of meter beyond the example. |
| Resonance and vocal timbre | Vocal-tract sound shaping; connects voice production to vowels and tone quality | **Partial:** [Voice production](../concepts/voice-production.md) mentions shaping. **Gap:** a usable resonance representation and timbre explanation. [NATS](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md) identifies the subject, not a completed lesson. |
| Registers range and transitions | Register distinctions and pitch span; connects phonation to repertoire choices | **Gap:** local explanations of registers, transitions and range. | Articulation vowels and diction | Formation of speech sounds and sung words; connects language to tone and musical time | **Gap:** local articulation and diction explanations. [Trinity](../references/trinity-2026-singing-syllabus.md) identifies diction as a technical subject. The Practice's spoken syllable is not a diction lesson. |
| Musical notation and sight singing | Relating written pitch and duration to sound; connects listening and rhythm to reading music | **Source only:** [Notation](../references/open-music-theory-undated-notation.md) and [Sight singing](../references/open-music-theory-undated-sight-singing.md). **Gap:** local notation and sight-singing instruction. The current Practice requires no notation and is not a sight-reading test. |
| Songs phrasing and expression | Organising a song's musical ideas and communicating its text and style; connects technique, musical understanding and performance | **Gap:** local repertoire, phrasing and interpretation explanations. The [Sight-singing Reference](../references/open-music-theory-undated-sight-singing.md) records a source basis for separating pitch and rhythm work. **Gap:** a fuller account of feedback and progress over time. |

## Connections across topics

The current Practice illustrates why overlap is useful. [Voice production](../concepts/voice-production.md) explains the sound being made; [Pitch](../concepts/pitch.md) explains its intended contour; [Beat and rhythm](../concepts/beat-and-rhythm.md) explains its timing. Stage4 brings them into one task while keeping the observations separate. The same documents therefore appear in several subject contexts without needing duplicate canonical files.

For a future song-learning Practice, the relevant questions would also include words, musical grouping and expression. Those gaps remain visible here rather than being filled with unsupported technique instructions. Selecting a next learning task belongs in the Practice and depends on its purpose and prerequisites, not on the position of a row in this table.

## Source basis and adaptation

- [NATS undergraduate voice-pedagogy syllabus](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md), course outcomes and schedule: the basis for the voice-function subjects. Its university teacher-training scope is broader than our learner target; its timetable is not adopted.
- [Trinity singing syllabus](../references/trinity-2026-singing-syllabus.md), selected technical-work, performance and sight-reading sections: connects vocal work with musicianship and expression. Our phrasing heading is a local grouping under musical interpretation, not a copied syllabus division. Exam grades and marking scales are not used as a learning sequence or self-assessment score.
- Existing Open Music Theory records on [notation](../references/open-music-theory-undated-notation.md), [rhythm](../references/open-music-theory-undated-notating-rhythm.md), [meter](../references/open-music-theory-undated-simple-meter.md) and [sight singing](../references/open-music-theory-undated-sight-singing.md): the package's recorded musical foundations. Western notation is a scoped account, not a representation of every singing tradition.
- [NIDCD voice care](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md): the existing voice-production and care basis, with the same limits on individual advice.

On 2026-10-14 the NATS syllabus and selected Trinity passages were inspected for this outline; their new Reference records state the extent. The relevant NIDCD HTML sections were also rechecked. No new full-text or media inspection is claimed for those records. This is an authored navigation update, not independent scientific or learner review.

## Maintaining this map

Choose subjects from established accounts and the intended Practice before attaching files. Explain each topic and its connections, then keep local links and coverage limits in the same row. Add a Reference when a newly adopted source structure needs provenance. Do not create a file merely to fill a role or a table cell. The five role folders remain the storage structure; the map is maintained Markdown and needs no generator.
