# Singing Body of Knowledge Outline

## Key takeaway

Understand singing through groups of small, connected systems, while keeping shared topics and missing explanations visible.

## Summary

This beginner outline separates organising groups, minimal subsystems, their components and relationships, and knowledge spanning several systems. Four groups locate ten candidate subsystems. Their boundaries are working explanatory choices grounded in established voice-pedagogy and music-theory subjects, not a universal partition of singing or a required number of Model files.

The outline defines what needs to be understood and to what depth. Concepts, Principles, Models, Methods and Practices remain the knowledge roles. A group is a navigation category; a Model represents a system; a Practice selects and combines knowledge for an outcome. Available activities remain in the [README](../README.md#start-here).

## Purpose and scope

**Audience:** beginners learning to understand singing and approach a suitable song. No notation reading, prior training or fixed vocal range is assumed. Learning by listening and imitation is included; Western notation and sight singing form an optional reading branch.

**Target:** explain what a singing task asks, distinguish pitch, timing, words and expression, and make informed learning choices. Required capabilities below are goals, not demonstrated learner results or a compulsory lesson sequence. The current Practices still implement only a first contour/rhythm task and single-reference pitch matching.

The existing [starting conditions](../practices/first-singing-session.md#starting-conditions) govern vocal activities. Do not sing through pain, hoarseness or unusual fatigue; persistent voice problems call for clinical advice. Comfort is an observation, not medical clearance. The [voice-care Reference](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) supplies the existing boundaries. This outline adds no vocal exercise or treatment.

Clinical diagnosis, rehabilitation, professional teacher preparation, exhaustive anatomy/acoustics, advanced range development, specialised belt/distortion techniques, professional production and comprehensive coverage of vocal traditions are outside this edition. Individual and developing voices, repertoire suitability and style remain required topics; they do not receive generic prescriptions here.

## How the hierarchy works

**Singing → group → minimal subsystem → components and relationships.** Topics spanning systems have a separate home under [shared coverage](#shared-coverage).

Here a **minimal subsystem** is a small, coherent explanatory unit for a stated question: it has a boundary, interacting components or defined relationships, and identifiable connections to neighbouring systems. It is not a claim of anatomical independence or an indivisible unit of nature. A single term such as pitch class is a component or Concept, not automatically a subsystem. A broad heading such as Vocal function is a group, not one minimal system.

The outline distinguishes physical functions, musical representations and coordination loops; they are different kinds of system. For example, Pitch relationships describes relations among musical pitches, while Phonation concerns sound generation. A grouping does not make them interchangeable. The candidate boundaries below may be refined when fuller accounts are inspected. Useful overlap is retained; neither one Model per row nor one file per term is required.

## Reading the coverage tables

**Understand** states the needed knowledge; **Be able to do** states introductory depth. **Partial** means an existing explanation contributes without completing the topic. **Source only** means a relevant external account is recorded but a locally sufficient explanation is missing. **Missing** identifies an unsupported local gap. A planned Model counts as missing until authored; a source label or passing structural check does not establish competence or scientific validation.

## Vocal function

This group organises physical functions. Separating them helps locate explanations, but does not assert independent operation. Body coordination and registration can span several functions.

### Respiration

**Boundary:** breath supply and its coordination with vocal demands; detailed laryngeal vibration belongs to Phonation. **Components and relationships:** respiratory movement, pressure and airflow. **Interface and example:** phrase demands connect musical grouping to breath use; explaining a short phrase should distinguish its breath requirement from its intended pitch. A complete respiratory Model is missing.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body and breath coordination | Movement, breathing and phrase demands; limits of posture and support metaphors | Describe the coordination a short phrase requires and distinguish an observation from a teacher's cue. | **Source only:** [NATS syllabus](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md). Local breathing and coordination explanations missing. |

### Phonation

**Boundary:** the generation, continuation and ending of voiced sound; the vocal tract shapes the resulting source. **Components and relationships:** vocal folds, airflow and vibration. **Interface and example:** an easy hum and a spoken vowel both involve voicing, but that does not establish identical articulation or healthy technique. Registration remains a shared topic rather than a synonym for pitch.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phonation and sound continuity | Starting, sustaining and ending voiced sound; relationship to airflow and articulation | Identify the intended start and release of a phrase and explain which parts of voice function are involved. | **Partial:** voice-production Concept; onset, release and continuity explanations missing. |

### Vocal tract resonance

**Boundary:** acoustic shaping, distinguished from source generation and from the movements that establish a tract configuration. **Components and relationships:** excitation, vocal-tract configuration, resonances and resulting spectrum. **Interface and example:** compare two vowels at the same intended pitch to ask which differences concern sound shaping. This is a proposed comparison, not a recording analysis or a full source–filter lesson.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source and vocal-tract shaping | Sound generation versus shaping, resonance and basic acoustic representation | Explain why voice production needs both a sound source and an account of shaping. | **Partial:** [Voice production](../concepts/voice-production.md). An acoustic Model and worked comparisons are missing. |
| Vowels and tone quality | Relationships among articulation, vocal-tract configuration and perceived timbre | Describe a change in vowel or tone without assuming that a sensation locates a sound source. | **Source only:** [NATS](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md). Local acoustic and perceptual explanations missing. |

### Articulation

**Boundary:** configurations and movements used to form speech sounds; text meaning and musical placement are handled at its interfaces. **Components and relationships:** tongue, lips, jaw and other articulators in vowel/consonant formation. **Interface and example:** a vowel change can involve articulation and resonance together; neither description replaces the other. Language-specific inventories still need sources.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vowels and consonants | Speech-sound categories, articulators and their roles in sung words | Distinguish a vowel-quality question from a consonant-formation question in a chosen word. | **Source only:** [NATS articulation](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md). No local speech-sound account. |

## Musical organisation

This group organises musical representations rather than anatomical organs. Initial pitch and tonal explanations use explicitly scoped Western conventions; other tuning systems and traditions require their own accounts.

### Pitch relationships

**Boundary:** the height, class and distance relationships of musical pitches under a declared convention. Tonal function and vocal production are outside this unit. **Components and relationships:** individual pitches, note labels, octave positions, octave-equivalent classes and intervals. **Interface and example:** A3 and A4 share a class but differ by an octave; a unison-matching task needs the height, not just the class. The [Pitch relationships Model](../models/pitch-relationships.md) supplies a focused representation.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch direction and contour | Higher, lower, repeated pitches and melodic shape | Describe a heard rise and return separately from whether either note matched a target. | **Partial:** [Pitch](../concepts/pitch.md) and [pitch-direction task](../practices/first-singing-session.md#stage2-explore-pitch-direction). The added audio examples contrast same, higher, lower and octave-related notes; subtle discrimination and broader melodic examples remain partial. |
| Pitch height and pitch class | Specific pitch, octave placement and class membership; pitch differs from loudness and timbre | Explain why an A-only display cannot establish a same-height match. | **Partial:** [Pitch Concept](../concepts/pitch.md) and [Pitch relationships Model](../models/pitch-relationships.md). No model of pitch perception or complete naming system is supplied. |
| Octaves and interval distance | Frequency ratios and equal-tempered step distance under the declared A4 reference | Compare unison, octave and a non-octave interval without confusing hertz difference with musical distance. | **Partial:** the pitch Model and [UNSW Reference](../references/wolfe-undated-note-frequencies.md). Full interval spelling, alternative tunings and perceptual discrimination remain outside the example. |

### Rhythmic organisation

**Boundary:** when sound events and silences occur and how long they last against a pulse; beat grouping belongs to Metrical organisation. **Components and relationships:** onsets, durations, rests, pulse and tempo. **Interface and example:** three hums and a silent fourth beat contain four pulse positions but only three vocal events. Changing the pitches need not change that timing pattern.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulse and tempo | Recurring beat, pace and maintaining a time reference | Keep a simple pulse distinct from the sounds placed within it and notice unintended speeding or slowing. | **Partial:** [Beat and rhythm](../concepts/beat-and-rhythm.md), [four-beat task](../practices/first-singing-session.md#stage3-feel-four-beats). More tempo examples needed. |
| Durations and rests | Relative note lengths, silence, subdivisions and rhythmic patterns | Explain or reproduce a short rhythm independently of its pitches. | **Partial:** beat-and-rhythm Concept; [rhythm Reference](../references/open-music-theory-undated-notating-rhythm.md). Subdivision and varied patterns missing locally. |

### Metrical organisation

**Boundary:** recurring grouping and subdivision of beats; a count alone does not establish an experienced meter. **Components and relationships:** beats, subdivisions, accent and grouping. **Interface and example:** four taps may support a four-beat grouping in context, but the beginner exercise alone does not establish every metrical interpretation. Rhythmic events and phrase boundaries connect to this unit.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meter and grouping | Beat groupings, accents, simple and compound organisation in the adopted tradition | Distinguish a grouping pattern from a mere count and relate it to phrase timing. | **Source only:** [Simple meter](../references/open-music-theory-undated-simple-meter.md); a local meter account and additional compound-meter sourcing are missing. |

### Tonal organisation

**Boundary:** contextual roles of pitches relative to a tonal centre, initially in a selected major-key account; pairwise distance belongs to Pitch relationships. **Components and relationships:** tonic, scale, scale degrees and their relations. **Interface and example:** A can be the tonic in A major or another scale degree in a different key. A note name alone does not specify its role. Tonal expectation and broader traditions remain explicit gaps.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intervals and tonal context | Relationships between pitches, musical patterns and a tonal reference in the selected repertoire | Identify a repeated or changed melodic relationship and explain how context helps anticipate a note. | **Partial:** [Pitch](../concepts/pitch.md) explains unison and octave placement with listening examples. The [pitch Model](../models/pitch-relationships.md) adds selected interval-distance comparisons; fuller interval naming and tonal context still need development; [Trinity](../references/trinity-2026-singing-syllabus.md) supplies assessment context only. |
| Tonal centre and scale degrees | A major scale and numbered positions relative to its tonic; note name differs from contextual role | Distinguish the pitch class A from its role as the first degree of A major. | **Source only:** [Open Music Theory major-scale account](../references/open-music-theory-undated-major-scales.md). A local tonal Model, minor/modal accounts and evidence for perceptual expectations remain missing. |

## Language music coordination

This group locates mappings between linguistic and musical representations. Articulation explains sound formation; the following subsystem asks how linguistic units are placed in music.

### Text setting

**Boundary:** the alignment of text with musical events, not the entire language or interpretation of a song. **Components and relationships:** syllables, word stress, consonant/vowel timing, note events and musical accents. **Interface and example:** a constructed two-syllable word can occupy two notes, or one syllable can extend over several; the mapping needs more information than a pitch list. Detailed accounts and language-specific examples still need source inspection.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text and musical timing | Coordination of consonants, vowel duration, syllables and musical accents | Explain how a word fits a rhythmic phrase while remaining intelligible. | **Missing:** no local text-setting or diction lesson. The Practice's spoken syllable is only a timing example. |

## Perception production coordination

This group locates bounded perception/action loops. The current unit concerns a single reference pitch; timing coordination, ensemble feedback and broader motor control are not silently included.

### Auditory vocal pitch matching

**Boundary:** one heard target, a vocal attempt and comparison feedback; not a complete theory of learning, hearing or the brain. **Components and relationships:** external reference, perceptual representation, sensorimotor translation, motor plan/output and auditory feedback. **Interface and example:** someone may hear that an attempt is lower yet struggle to change it. That observation does not diagnose which process caused the mismatch. The [existing Model](../models/auditory-vocal-pitch-matching.md) is a bounded subset of a proposed published architecture.

| Topic | Understand — required knowledge | Be able to do — introductory depth | Existing explanations and gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reference-pitch matching | Intended pitch, an external reference, octave placement and comparison | Explain the difference between following a contour and matching a reference; identify what comparison evidence is needed. | **Partial:** [Match a reference pitch](../practices/match-a-reference-pitch.md), the [comparison Method](../methods/reference-pitch-comparison.md) and selectable reference audio supply a single-note task. Independent matching results and broader transfer are not demonstrated. |
| Intonation and listening feedback | Intended versus produced pitch, sustained or changing notes, and uncertainty in auditory or device feedback | Describe where a phrase differs from its reference without reducing its musical quality to one pitch score. | **Partial:** [Intonation](../concepts/intonation.md), the [comparison Method](../methods/reference-pitch-comparison.md) and [auditory vocal Model](../models/auditory-vocal-pitch-matching.md) distinguish target, output and uncertainty. Phrase-level, expressive and context-dependent intonation remain unimplemented. |

## Interfaces between systems

| Connection | Relationship to understand | Boundary or gap |
|---|---|---|
| Respiration ↔ Phonation | Airflow and pressure relate breath supply to vibration | No single generic breathing prescription follows from the connection |
| Articulation ↔ Vocal-tract resonance | Movements establish configurations whose acoustic effects need a separate description | A reported sensation is not a measured resonance |
| Pitch relationships ↔ Auditory-vocal pitch matching | The musical target specifies the relation to compare; the control loop concerns hearing and producing it | A correct calculation does not establish accurate perception or production |
| Rhythmic organisation ↔ Metrical organisation | Events occupy pulse positions within possible recurring groupings | Four counts alone do not prove a complete meter analysis |
| Pitch relationships ↔ Tonal organisation | Pitch classes and intervals participate in contextual musical roles | A shared pitch class is not a shared scale degree across every key |
| Articulation ↔ Text setting ↔ Rhythmic organisation | Speech sounds and syllables must be related to musical timing | Pronunciation, alignment and expressive emphasis remain distinguishable |
| Phrase structure ↔ Respiration and registration | Song demands constrain useful coordination questions and repertoire choices | A momentary note does not establish suitability for the whole song |
| Notation ↔ Musical representations | Written labels and symbols encode selected pitch and timing information | Optional literacy is not a substitute for listening or vocal coordination |

These are explanatory connections, not a fixed processing pipeline. A missed note or entry can have several causes; its location in a song does not identify the cause.

## Maintaining the outline
