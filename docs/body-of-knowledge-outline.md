# Singing Body of Knowledge Outline

## Key takeaway

Define the knowledge a beginner needs to understand singing, learn a suitable song and make informed practice choices. Keep required topics visible even when no local explanation exists yet.

## Summary

This outline specifies intended coverage across voice function, musicianship, words, performance and learning. It adapts established subject structures to a beginner-facing project, then maps existing explanations and gaps against concrete learning outcomes. The ten areas and their subdivisions are our selected organisation, not a universal singing taxonomy or an examination syllabus.

Coverage tables distinguish required understanding and intended capabilities from the explanations currently available. A topic remains necessary even when its support is partial or missing. Concepts, Principles, Models, Methods and Practices remain the knowledge roles; several topics may share an explanation. Use the [README](../README.md#start-here) for available learning tasks.

## Purpose and scope

**Audience and starting point:** beginners learning to understand singing and approach a suitable song. No notation reading, prior training or fixed vocal range is assumed.

**Target:** help a beginner understand what a singing task asks, learn a short suitable song, distinguish pitch, timing, words and expression, and choose a useful next practice step. Learning by listening and imitation is included. **Optional reading branch:** Western notation and sight singing support learners who choose to read scores. They are not entry requirements or a representation of every musical tradition; listening and imitation remain an included route.

Expected depth is stated as an observable understanding or capability in each row. Some outcomes require comparison or explanation; others involve applying knowledge to an appropriate task. They are intended learning outcomes, not a prescribed exercise sequence, a fixed vocal range, a grading scale or a promise of improvement. A Practice must provide its own prerequisites, procedure and support before a vocal activity is introduced.

The existing [starting conditions](../practices/first-singing-session.md#starting-conditions) continue to govern the first session. Do not sing through pain, hoarseness or unusual fatigue; persistent voice problems call for clinical advice. Comfort is a useful observation, not medical clearance. This outline introduces no new vocal exercise or treatment. The [voice-care Reference](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) records the health guidance behind those boundaries.

This selected coverage is a beginner foundation. Clinical diagnosis and rehabilitation, professional teacher preparation, comprehensive anatomy or acoustics, advanced range development, specialised belt/distortion/extended techniques, professional production, and exhaustive coverage of vocal traditions are outside this edition. Teacher-supported exploration is not replaced by generic self-instruction. Age-specific training and specialised styles need their own scope and sources before procedures are added.

## Reading the coverage tables

**Understand** states the required knowledge; **Be able to do** sets intended introductory depth. These are coverage goals, not recorded learner outcomes. Existing explanations and gaps show what support is available. **Partial** means a local explanation or Practice contributes but does not fulfil the whole row. **Source only** means an external account is recorded but the local lesson is missing. **Missing** means no focused local explanation has been identified. These are coverage descriptions, not measures of singing ability or evidence of completed practice.

## Connections across areas

A singing task can combine several subjects. These connections explain shared coverage without making the areas a fixed sequence or requiring a separate document for every overlap.

| Connected areas | Relationship to understand |
|---|---|
| [Voice production and breathing](#voice-production-and-breathing) ↔ [Songs, phrasing and performance](#songs-phrasing-and-performance) | Phrase length, intended starts and releases, and breath opportunities connect vocal coordination with musical grouping. |
| [Articulation, vowels and diction](#articulation-vowels-and-diction) ↔ [Resonance and vocal timbre](#resonance-and-vocal-timbre) | Vowel articulation contributes to vocal-tract shaping and tone quality; a descriptive sensation does not establish a mechanism. |
| [Listening, pitch and intonation](#listening-pitch-and-intonation) ↔ [Practice, feedback and skill development](#practice-feedback-and-skill-development) | Hearing a target, producing a note and judging the comparison are distinct parts of a task. One attempt does not establish retention or transfer. |
| [Articulation, vowels and diction](#articulation-vowels-and-diction) ↔ [Beat, rhythm and meter](#beat-rhythm-and-meter) | Syllables, consonants and vowel duration must be considered alongside pulse, musical duration and accent when explaining text timing. |
| [Registers, range and transitions](#registers-range-and-transitions) ↔ [Songs, phrasing and performance](#songs-phrasing-and-performance) | A song's pitch placement and sustained demands inform suitability; touching one note does not establish that a whole song is appropriate. |
| [Notation and sight singing](#notation-and-sight-singing) ↔ [Listening, pitch and intonation](#listening-pitch-and-intonation) and [Beat, rhythm and meter](#beat-rhythm-and-meter) | The optional reading branch connects written symbols with pitch and timing. Reading, listening and vocal-production difficulties remain distinguishable. |
| [Vocal care and comfortable use](#vocal-care-and-comfortable-use) ↔ [Practice, feedback and skill development](#practice-feedback-and-skill-development) | Task selection includes the existing starting and stop conditions, total voice use and limits of self-observation. |

A missed entry could involve rhythm, text preparation or the cue being followed; its location in a song does not determine its cause. Coverage links provide ways to investigate a question, not evidence that a proposed explanation has been demonstrated.

## Maintaining the outline

Use established accounts and intended Practices to identify needed topics and depth, then map the available explanations. Keep missing topics visible and source disagreements identifiable. Do not create one file per row or one object of every role. Review future vocal procedures separately from this coverage plan; preserve actual learner observations as such and keep private recordings out of the public package.

This is the main coverage guide. The README introduces the current package, and Practices own task sequence and entry conditions. The outline is maintained Markdown and needs no generator.

Keep the ten-area topic list, coverage rows and overview aligned as scope changes. Additional subtopic pictures remain optional and chosen by the user. Update any retained images when their labels or claims change; embed them without separate links to open the same asset.
