# Singing Body of Knowledge Outline

## Key takeaway

Define the knowledge a beginner needs to understand singing, learn a suitable song and make informed practice choices. Keep required topics visible even when no local explanation exists yet.

## Summary

This outline specifies intended coverage across voice function, musicianship, words, performance and learning. It adapts established subject structures to a beginner-facing project, then maps existing explanations and gaps against concrete learning outcomes. The ten areas and their subdivisions are our selected organisation, not a universal singing taxonomy or an examination syllabus.

The implemented package still contains three Concepts and [First singing session](../practices/first-singing-session.md): comfortable voice awareness, pitch direction, beat and a short humming task. The broader outcomes below describe future coverage, not lessons already supplied or competence already demonstrated. Concepts, Principles, Models, Methods and Practices remain the knowledge roles; several topics may share an explanation.

## Purpose and scope

**Learning target:** understand singing, learn a short suitable song and make informed practice choices.

This beginner foundation assumes no notation reading or previous vocal training. It includes learning by listening and imitation, with repertoire, language and style chosen for the learner. Area 8, Western notation and sight singing, is an optional reading branch. The other areas contribute to the foundation at the depth specified below; learners do not need to complete them all before beginning a supported task.

Advanced vocal techniques, clinical care, professional teacher training and exhaustive coverage of musical traditions are outside this edition. Individual voices and specialised styles require appropriately scoped guidance. See [scope boundaries](#connections-and-scope-boundaries) for detail.

## Intended practices and depth

The target is to help a beginner understand what a singing task asks, learn a short suitable song, distinguish pitch, timing, words and expression, and choose a useful next practice step. Learning by listening and imitation is included. Western notation is an additional reading skill, not an entry requirement or a representation of every musical tradition.

Each coverage row separates **Understand** (the required knowledge) from **Be able to do** (the intended capability at beginner depth). Capabilities include explaining, listening, making a choice and performing a suitable task. These are authored development goals, not a prescribed exercise sequence, a fixed vocal range, a grading scale or a promise of improvement. A Practice must provide its own prerequisites, procedure and support before a vocal activity is introduced.

The existing [starting conditions](../practices/first-singing-session.md#starting-conditions) continue to govern the first session. Do not sing through pain, hoarseness or unusual fatigue; persistent voice problems call for clinical advice. Comfort is a useful observation, not medical clearance. This outline introduces no new vocal exercise or treatment. The [voice-care Reference](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) records the health guidance behind those boundaries.

## Practice pathway and development priorities

The following is a proposed development path toward learning a song. Only the first Practice is available in this edition. Later entries describe work to author, not exercises ready to follow or a compulsory curriculum. Rhythm work can develop alongside pitch work; notation is an optional branch.

| Practice target | Knowledge it brings together | Availability and next work |
|---|---|---|
| [First singing session](../practices/first-singing-session.md) — observe comfort, pitch direction and a simple pulse | [Voice production](#voice-production-and-breathing), [care](#vocal-care-and-comfortable-use), [pitch](#listening-pitch-and-intonation), [rhythm](#beat-rhythm-and-meter), [reflection](#practice-feedback-and-skill-development) | **Available:** a first contour-and-rhythm task. It does not teach reference-pitch matching. |
| Match a reference pitch — attempt a comfortable note and compare with its reference | Listening, pitch, intonation, voice coordination and feedback | **Proposed; first development priority:** inspect claim-specific sources, explain pitch and octave comparisons, supply usable reference audio and write guided comparison steps. |
| Reproduce a short rhythm — maintain a pulse while reproducing durations and rests | Beat, rhythm, meter and feedback | **Proposed:** develop pulse, subdivision and grouping explanations with audible examples and a focused Practice. Can proceed alongside pitch matching. |
| Learn a short melody by ear — combine pitch relationships and timing | Pitch matching, intervals, rhythm, suitable pitch placement and practice methods | **Proposed:** add interval/context explanations, melody references and guidance for separating and combining difficulties. |
| Learn and sing a short suitable song — combine melody, words and expression | Repertoire suitability, voice coordination, diction, phrasing, style and reflection | **Proposed:** develop song-selection guidance and supported accounts of range/tessitura, coordination, pronunciation and phrasing before writing the integrated Practice. |

Develop supporting explanations and the relevant Practice together. Address care and task suitability within every new vocal Practice. Use Concepts, Principles, Models and Methods where they help the reader; a topic does not require one file of each type. The wider 34-topic scope remains visible even when it is not part of the next development task.

## Reading the coverage tables

Each row states what to **understand** and **be able to do**, then maps existing explanations and gaps. The capability defines the intended beginner depth; it is a target for future learning support, not a claim that a reader can already achieve it. The [Practice pathway](#practice-pathway-and-development-priorities) shows how topics combine around practical targets.

**Partial** means a local explanation or Practice contributes but does not fulfil the whole row. **Source only** means an external account is recorded but the local lesson is missing. **Missing** means no focused local explanation has been identified. These are coverage descriptions, not measures of singing ability or evidence of completed practice.

## Connections and scope boundaries

| Connection | Why the outline links these areas |
|---|---|
| Voice production, resonance and registers | Explain sound generation, shaping and changes across a suitable phrase as connected aspects of singing. |
| Listening, rhythm and notation | Relate intended sounds to pitch and time; notation adds a way to represent and read them when relevant. |
| Diction, resonance and phrasing | Connect sung words, vowel choices and musical timing with the meaning of a phrase. |
| Song selection, range/tessitura and style | Judge the demands of a particular song in relation to the learner and musical context. |
| Vocal care and practice feedback across all areas | Bring starting conditions, observations, support and next-step decisions into each Practice. |

Learning a short song can require listening, timing, words, vocal coordination and interpretation together. A missed entry could involve rhythm, text preparation or the cue being followed; its location in a song does not determine its cause. The outline keeps these subjects distinguishable while allowing a Practice to combine them for a particular question.

The existing first session illustrates a smaller combination: voice production explains the sound, pitch explains its intended contour, and beat/rhythm explains timing. These remain separate observations in the Practice. Its rise-and-return task establishes neither accurate pitch matching nor competence in reading or performing a song.

The required coverage above is a selected beginner foundation. Clinical diagnosis and rehabilitation, professional teacher preparation, comprehensive anatomy or acoustics, advanced range development, specialised belt/distortion/extended techniques, professional production, and exhaustive coverage of vocal traditions are outside this edition. Teacher-supported exploration is not replaced by generic self-instruction. Age-specific training and specialised styles need their own scope and sources before procedures are added.

## Maintaining the outline

Use established accounts and intended Practices to identify needed topics and depth, then map the available explanations. Keep missing topics visible and source disagreements identifiable. Do not create one file per row or one object of every role. Review future vocal procedures separately from this coverage plan; preserve actual learner observations as such and keep private recordings out of the public package.
