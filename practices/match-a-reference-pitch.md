# Match a reference pitch

## Key takeaway

Listen to an easy reference, make a brief attempt, compare it and record approximately matching, higher, lower or uncertain before choosing what to do next.

## Summary

This ear-first task requires only a phone or computer that can play the bundled audio. A microphone, recorder, tuner, piano, notation reading and note naming are optional or unnecessary. The output is a reference ID, an honest comparison and a next action, not a guaranteed accurate note. Completing the observation loop with uncertain is legitimate; it does not mean pitch matching has been mastered.

**Pitch** means how high or low a sound seems. A **reference** is the target sound you choose. Match its height, not its electronic tone colour, volume or length. An **octave** is a higher or lower version with the same note letter: A3 and A4 are related but not the same height. This task aims at the same height, sometimes called a unison.

## Starting conditions

Choose a quiet place, comfortable playback volume and normal playback speed. Use your usual easy speaking volume for vocal attempts, not a whisper or shout. Do not begin vocal work while hoarse, ill with voice changes, painful or unusually tired. Stop for pain, strain, new hoarseness or effortful speaking. Do not sweep to your range extremes, hold your breath, force a note or extend a sound until breath runs out. Suspected or persistent voice problems call for a clinician; a singing teacher helps with musical coordination. These general [NIDCD boundaries](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) are not personal medical clearance.

Stages give a **recommended** order. Clear playback and comfortable vocal use are **prerequisites** for attempting a note, not abilities proved by finishing the previous Practice. You may do the listening parts without singing, or stop at any stage. Durations and suggested retry limits below are conservative task-design choices, not a scientifically established safe dose.

## Stage1 Set up and choose a reference

1. Open a reference below. On GitHub, use the file's **Raw** or download control if a player does not appear, then open the saved MP3 in the device's audio player. Playback needs no account, microphone permission or paid app. Let one file finish before starting another. Offline use means saving the desired files first.
2. Listen at a low, clearly audible volume. If silent, distorted or unclear, check the device's mute and output settings without singing. Stop this task if clear playback cannot be obtained; do not compensate by singing or playing louder.
3. Say a comfortable sentence and make one brief easy hum, if comfortable. Listen to A3 as a starting audition only, not a note everyone should sing. If it seems far above your easy hum, audition a lower option; if far below, audition a higher one. You do not need to know the pitch of your hum.
4. Select a reference that seems near an easy part of your voice. Make at most a brief tentative attempt to check comfort. If it requires reaching, squeezing or forcing, stop that attempt. Pick a different reference or finish with **no comfortable reference found**. Do not sing the whole bank as a range test. Write the chosen file ID.

## Stage2 Hear the comparison words

1. Without singing, play A3 twice using the table: the second sound has the same pitch. Then play A3 followed by E4: the second is higher. Reverse those files: the second is lower. Replay only as needed; these are large, obvious examples, not a fine-hearing test.
2. Play A2 then A3. The second is an octave higher. Both names contain A, but they are not a same-height match. You are not asked to sing this octave leap.
3. If the examples do not sound clearly distinguishable, mark **comparison uncertain**. You may make one comfortable vocal attempt later, but do not invent a direction or repeatedly correct something you cannot judge. Listening only and finishing with uncertainty is also acceptable.

The [Pitch relationships Model](../models/pitch-relationships.md) explains the comparison: A3 and A4 share class A, but A4 is twelve semitones above A3. A unison task needs the same height; a shared letter or a similar rise-and-return contour is insufficient. This explanation adds no new vocal attempt.

When comparing your attempt below, higher and lower will mean **your voice relative to the reference**. Tone colour or loudness differences alone do not answer that question.

## Stage3 Listen and reproduce one note

1. Return to the selected reference, listen while silent, and let the file end. Hold the height briefly in mind. Replay it if you lose the target; no perfect memory is required.
2. On an ordinary breath, gently hum mm or sing a comfortable oo for about one second, aiming for that height. Release and breathe normally. The suggested second is only a short-attempt cue; stop sooner for discomfort. You need not reproduce the full recording length.
3. Pause. Do not stretch a note or sing over a looping reference until it blends. If the attempt felt uncomfortable, stop vocal work and go to the record; comfort takes precedence over completing a comparison.

The task involves hearing, retaining a target and organising a vocal action. Trouble with one attempt does not reveal which part was difficult. That distinction is explained in the [auditory vocal Model](../models/auditory-vocal-pitch-matching.md); no diagnosis is inferred here.

## Stage4 Compare and choose the next step

1. Replay the reference while silent. Compare it with the reasonably steady part of your attempt, ignoring the start and release. A brief crossing of the target during a slide does not establish a steady match.
2. Select one report below. When unsure of direction, octave, memory or steadiness, choose **uncertain**, not approximately matching by default.
3. Follow the corresponding action. After a clear small mismatch, one further comfortable attempt is enough for this first session. If repeated comparison is unclear, stop rather than accumulating repetitions. A new target must be recorded as new; changing it does not retroactively make the old attempt correct.

| Voice relative to reference | What it means here | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Approximately matching | No clear height difference is heard and the octave seems the same; an ear judgment, not a measured pass | Keep the report provisional; stop, or verify once after a pause if comfortable |
| Higher | The voice seems above the target | For a small difference, aim a little lower on one fresh attempt; for a large or suspected octave difference, recheck comfort and reference choice instead of forcing down |
| Lower | The voice seems below the target | For a small difference, aim a little higher on one fresh attempt; for a large or suspected octave difference, recheck comfort and reference choice instead of pushing up |
| Uncertain | Direction, octave, memory of the sound or the steady portion cannot be judged | Listen again without singing; if still unclear, record uncertainty and finish; optional recording or later human feedback may help |

**Optional recording:** Record only when convenient on the same device. A reference, pause, attempt and reference replay should be separate audible events. If playback interrupts recording, record the attempt alone and alternate listening, or skip recording. Listen at normal speed and keep the recording private. One device is enough; recording is not a prerequisite.

**Optional tuner:** First play the reference alone and check that the displayed Hz agrees approximately with the table. Stop playback before singing. Compare the steady portion of your voice, including octave or frequency, not only a letter or colour. A jumping, doubled/halved or conflicting reading stays uncertain. Do not force the voice to follow a screen. More detail is in the [comparison Method](../methods/reference-pitch-comparison.md); no tool is required here.

**Constructed decisions, not learner results:** A comfortable attempt that clearly sounds slightly higher leads to a small downward retry. An attempt that sounds related but may be an octave lower leads to uncertain and a reference check, not an automatic pass. Pain leads to stopping regardless of whether the note sounded right.

## Stage5 Record and finish

1. Fill one row in the blank record. Use live listening unless recording or a tuner was actually used. Mark not attempted and the reason when a prerequisite prevented singing; do not invent a pitch result.
2. Write the next action and stop the session. A future attempt with the same reference can check repeatability, but today's result does not establish retention, transfer to songs or general singing ability.
3. Keep filled records and personal recordings outside this public repository unless you deliberately authorise publication. The reference files are synthetic; your own audio may identify you.

## Source basis and boundaries

[Blanco 2021](../references/blanco-2021-pitch-matching-feedback.md) supplies a published reference-replay procedure and evidence that feedback conditions matter. Our ear-first task is not its full laboratory protocol; repeated listening alone is not promised to improve accuracy. The [NATS syllabus](../references/nats-undated-voice-pedagogy-syllabus.md), page 2, places hearing, perception and motor learning within pedagogy; [Trinity](../references/trinity-2026-singing-syllabus.md), page 69, uses a given reference within broader singing tests. Both were re-inspected on 2026-10-14, including their relevant table/page. Neither validates this task or imposes an exam grade.

Reference selection, short attempts, qualitative labels and retry limits are authored applications. [Pitch](../concepts/pitch.md), [Intonation](../concepts/intonation.md) and the linked Method and Model retain the meanings, evidence and limitations that support them. Coverage remains limited to one note, with no song-level intonation, range extension or clinical training claimed.
