# Voice production

## Key takeaway

Voiced sound begins with air from the lungs setting the vocal folds in motion and is shaped by the vocal tract.

## Summary

The vocal folds are in the larynx. During voicing, air from the lungs sets them vibrating; the throat, mouth and nose shape the resulting sound. This basic account explains where a sung hum comes from, not how to diagnose or optimise an individual voice.

## Meaning

The **larynx** is a structure in the neck that contains the **vocal folds**, two bands of tissue. During quiet breathing the folds are apart. During voicing they approach one another and vibrate as air passes through. The resulting sound is modified by spaces of the throat, mouth and nose. Speaking and singing both use this sound production system.

The voice's pitch and loudness are different properties of the result. Making a pitch higher does not mean that the singer must make it louder.

## Example and boundary

You can gently hum one comfortable sound and then speak a short sentence. Both involve voiced sound; the audible difference does not prove that you used a correct or incorrect singing technique.

This description is a general anatomical explanation. It does not prescribe a breathing manoeuvre, assess a singer's vocal folds or establish a training effect. The separate [First singing session](../practices/first-singing-session.md) applies it conservatively.
