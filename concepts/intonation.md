# Intonation

## Key takeaway

Intonation describes how a performed pitch relates to its intended pitch; confidence in the comparison is a separate observation.

## Summary

Here intonation means pitch accuracy relative to a chosen reference. A voice can sound pleasant yet be higher or lower than that reference. A stable note can be consistently misplaced, and a moving note may pass through the target without staying near it. One pitch judgment is not a verdict on someone's musical ability.

## Meaning in this task

**Sharp** means higher than the intended pitch; **flat** means lower. These words describe a relative discrepancy here, not the sharp or flat symbols in a note name. The comparison direction always describes **your voice relative to the reference**, not the reference relative to your voice.

Listen to the reasonably steady part after the start. Do not count a brief crossing while sliding up or down as a sustained match. If the pitch wanders or the relevant part cannot be identified, report **uncertain** and describe the movement rather than forcing one direction.

## Four useful reports

| Report | Meaning and limit |
|---|---|
| Approximately matching | After comparison, no clear height difference is heard and the octave seems the same. This is a provisional ear judgment, not measured accuracy. |
| Higher | The sung note seems above the reference. It may be a small difference or an octave; these are not equally small corrections. |
| Lower | The sung note seems below the reference. Do not infer its cause from the direction. |
| Uncertain | The comparison, octave, memory of the note or sound quality is too unclear to decide. This is valid information, not a failed test. |

These labels are this package's reporting convention. There is no universal numerical pass boundary hidden behind approximately matching.

## Constructed comparison

Suppose a target is A3 at 220 Hz. A hypothetical steady voice at 225 Hz is higher; 215 Hz is lower; 440 Hz is an octave higher, despite also having the letter A. These invented frequencies illustrate direction, not a recording analysis or what an untrained listener can necessarily discriminate.

For optional measurement, a **cent** is one hundredth of an equal-tempered semitone. The signed difference is 1200 times log base 2 of (voice frequency / reference frequency). Positive means higher; negative means lower. This calculation describes a selected measured portion; it neither sets our ear-only passing standard nor judges an entire performance.

## Evidence and limits

[Blanco and colleagues](../references/blanco-2021-pitch-matching-feedback.md) quantify error against reference notes and use a study-specific correctness boundary. [UNSW](../references/wolfe-undated-note-frequencies.md) explains frequency ratios and cents. Study cutoffs are not imported as beginner grades. Auditory judgment, device estimates and singing in musical context have different limits. A tuner cannot establish vocal comfort, expression or the reason for a mismatch.
