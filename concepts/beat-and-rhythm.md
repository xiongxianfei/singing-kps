# Beat and rhythm

## Key takeaway

A beat is a recurring pulse; rhythm concerns how musical sounds and silences occur in time.

## Summary

You can tap a steady beat while making sounds on some beats and staying silent on others. Beat and rhythm are related but not identical: a repeated pulse provides an organising reference, while note durations and rests create a rhythmic pattern.

## Meaning

In the Western music theory terminology used here, a **beat** is a recurring pulse that you might tap with your finger. **Rhythm** concerns the durations of sounds and rests. A **rest** is an intended span of silence; it is not a forgotten note.

A chosen tempo tells you approximately how quickly beats recur. For this beginner task, no precise number of beats per minute is required.

## Worked example

Tap four evenly spaced beats while counting **one, two, three, four**. Say a quiet spoken **la** on beats one, two and three, then remain silent on beat four. You maintained a four-beat pulse and performed a three-sound pattern followed by a rest. Singing can later add different pitches to the first three sounds.

If the tapping speeds up, slow down before adding pitches. A successful count does not by itself show that your sung pitches were accurate.
