# Singing KPS

## Key takeaway

Begin with one comfortable, observable singing task rather than memorising an entire singing curriculum.

## Summary

Singing KPS is an independent, source-linked beginner learning package using the Knowledge Practice System authoring language 9.x and the inspected Core 11.0.0 conventions. It begins with one short humming and rhythm exercise. No musical notation, prior training, fixed vocal range or performance experience is assumed.

## Start here

Read [First singing session](practices/first-singing-session.md). It offers a gentle three-note contour over four beats, with explicit stop conditions and a simple way to decide what to practise next. An optional voice recorder may help you listen back; the exercise does not require a keyboard or an app.

Do not practise through pain, hoarseness, illness-related voice changes or persistent vocal fatigue. The [NIDCD voice care guidance](references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) identifies warning signs and when to seek professional help.

## Knowledge coverage

Use the [Body of Knowledge Outline](docs/body-of-knowledge-outline.md) to understand what this subject needs to cover and how deeply. Required topics stay visible even when no local explanation exists. The Practice supplies activity order and entry conditions. This initial package currently includes:

- [Voice production](concepts/voice-production.md) explains how voiced sound is generated and shaped.
- [Pitch](concepts/pitch.md) explains higher and lower sounds without requiring note names.
- [Beat and rhythm](concepts/beat-and-rhythm.md) separates the pulse from durations and silence.
- [First singing session](practices/first-singing-session.md) integrates these ideas into a small task.

The Principles, Models and Methods directories are intentionally empty in this initial slice. KPS does not require manufacturing one object per role.

## Evidence and limits

The external References record what was inspected and what the sources do not establish. The exercise order, three-note pattern and feedback checklist are authored KPS applications; they are not a clinically validated protocol or a guarantee of singing improvement. This repo does not assess voice disorders or replace personalised instruction.
