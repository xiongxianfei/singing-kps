# Reference pitch comparison

## Key takeaway

Compare a short vocal attempt with the same reference before deciding whether or how to adjust it.

## Summary

This is a reusable listen, attempt and replay comparison. It adapts the vocal reference-replay operation reported by Blanco and colleagues, not their complete laboratory training protocol. The ear-first application records direction and uncertainty; a recording or pitch display can supplement but is not required for the operation.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Use a playable, identified reference, a quiet setting and a pitch that can be attempted without strain. No piano, microphone, tuner, paid app or notation knowledge is required. The phone or computer plays the reference at normal speed. Stop vocal work for pain, hoarseness or unusual fatigue; comfort alone is not medical clearance. A reference that sounds distorted or cannot be replayed is not a usable comparison input.

## Rationale

The study's baseline allowed another decision after replaying the target. The useful comparison is between produced and intended pitch, not between their loudness or timbre. Replaying restores an external anchor; it does not guarantee that a beginner can hear the error. Our short attempts, four report labels and uncertainty exit are local teaching adaptations.

## Procedure

1. Identify the reference file. Listen while silent, then let playback finish. Keep the intended height in mind; no note naming is necessary.
2. On a normal breath, make one brief, easy hum or vowel sound aiming for that height. Stop rather than holding it until you run out of breath. Do not copy an electronic tone colour.
3. Replay the same reference while silent. Compare its height with the reasonably steady part of the attempt. Use **approximately matching**, **higher**, **lower** or **uncertain** to describe the voice relative to the reference. Include uncertainty about an octave or an unstable note.
4. If a small direction difference is clear and the voice remains comfortable, make one small correction after a pause and compare again. If unclear, replay once without singing; if still unclear, retain uncertain and stop or seek additional feedback. Do not alternate random corrections.

The [Match a reference pitch Practice](../practices/match-a-reference-pitch.md) supplies audio selection, worked listening comparisons and a blank record. The operation above remains usable without opening that link.

## Optional feedback

**Recording:** If the same device can record its speaker and your voice, capture a reference, silence, an attempt and a replay as distinct events. Otherwise record your attempt and alternate it with reference playback; a second device is not required. If the recorder interrupts playback, skip recording rather than delaying the task. Listen back at normal speed, identify the attempt clearly and keep personal audio outside the public repository. Different playback tone colour alone is not a pitch error.

**Pitch display:** A compatible tuner may offer a cross-check, not a compulsory score. Play the chosen reference alone to check its displayed frequency, then stop playback before singing so the microphone is not measuring the speaker. Compare a reasonably steady portion, including octave or Hz, not just a note letter or a green indicator. A jumping display, disagreement with listening, background sound or a doubled/halved reading warrants uncertain, not forced vocal adjustment. Some octave number conventions differ; the file's Hz identifies the target.

## Checks and counterevidence

The output names the reference, report, evidence mode and next action. Approximately matching is provisional without independent comparison. A familiar sound, correct note letter alone, or repeated confident self-ratings does not establish an exact match. A suspected octave error needs a different decision from a small drift. Failure to hear the error cannot locate a hearing, memory or vocal-control problem.

## Limits and source alignment

[Blanco 2021](../references/blanco-2021-pitch-matching-feedback.md), section 2.3, supports the reference-replay procedure. Its laboratory study also found that feedback conditions matter; it does not establish that repetition alone or our ear-only adaptation produces lasting improvement. Its range sweeps, long attempts, octave-normalised scoring, correctness cutoff and training dose are not adopted. [NIDCD](../references/nidcd-2025-taking-care-of-your-voice.md) supports general stop boundaries, not individual safety clearance.
